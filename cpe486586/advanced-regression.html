What to watch

Training error falls as the degree rises. Held out error falls, reaches a minimum, then climbs. The gap between the two curves is the cost of fitting noise.

How to read the four plots

Residuals against fitted values. A horizontal band of constant width supports linearity and equal variance. Curvature points to a missing term. A widening spread points to unequal variance.

Normal quantile plot. Points on the straight line support normal errors. A bend at both ends shows heavy tails, and a bend at one end shows skew.

Lag plot. A slope through the cloud shows that neighbouring errors carry information about each other. The Durbin-Watson statistic near 2 indicates no first order correlation.

What to watch

The cloud of estimates stretches along a line of slope −1 as the correlation grows. One coefficient can be raised and the other lowered with little change in the fitted values, so the data give weak information about each one separately.

The sum of the two coefficients stays well determined. Prediction at predictor values inside the observed cloud stays accurate, and the individual coefficients are the quantities that suffer.

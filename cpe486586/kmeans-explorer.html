K-means, step by step

K-means run one step at a time on data you can change. The assignment step sends a point to its nearest centroid. The update step moves a centroid to the mean of the points it won. The objective J falls until no point switches cluster, and the run stops there.

The method in four lines

1.   ci = arg minj ‖xi − μj‖2
Assignment. A point joins its nearest centroid. Dropping the square root leaves the arg min unchanged, so the code compares squared distances.
2.   μj = 1nj ∑i : ci = j xi
Update. A centroid moves to the mean of the points assigned to it. A centroid that wins no points stays where it is, and the state panel reports it as empty.
3.   J = ∑i ‖xi − μci‖2
The objective, the within-cluster sum of squares. The assignment step lowers it for fixed centroids and the update step lowers it for fixed assignments. Neither can raise it.
4.   stop when no ci changes
J takes finitely many values, so the loop ends. It ends at a local minimum, and the starting centroids decide which one.

The arithmetic of the last update

What to watch

The default run drops three centroids anywhere in the box. μ2 starts beside the right-hand group and ends on the top group, while μ3 climbs in to take its place. The red rings mark points that switched at the last assignment. The run needs ten updates to settle.

The slide's eleven points with the slide's two centroids settle after one update, at (1.80, 1.40) and (5.83, 5.67). Those starting centroids already sit in different groups, so the first assignment is final. Use that example to check the arithmetic panel.

Choose Points as the start and press New start a few times. Most starts end at the same J. Now and then two centroids share one group, and the strip of ten starts counts how often.

Then try the stretched groups and the two moons. K-means puts its boundaries halfway between centroids, so its regions are convex. Restarting won't help it follow a long or curved group.

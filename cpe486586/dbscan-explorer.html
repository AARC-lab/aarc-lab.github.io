DBSCAN, step by step

DBSCAN builds clusters out of dense regions, one stage at a time. Points start brown and turn red or green once their neighbors within ε are counted. A cluster takes a fixed color when it is complete, and the points left over become gray noise.

Neighbor counts points within ε, the point itself excluded

What to watch

A point is core exactly when its distance to the minPts-th neighbor is at most ε. The yellow ε line on the k-distance plot therefore splits the sorted curve. Non-core points sit on the left in the green band, above the line, and core points sit on the right in the red band.

Open the uneven densities. An ε small enough to keep the two tight groups apart leaves most of the diffuse group as noise. Raise it until the diffuse group survives, and the tight groups merge.

The method in five lines

1.   Nε(i) = { j ≠ i : ‖xi − xj‖ ≤ ε }
Count the neighbors inside a circle of radius ε. A point counts when its center lies inside the circle. A dot that only touches the edge doesn't.
2.   i is core  ⇔  |Nε(i)| ≥ minPts
Points with at least minPts neighbors are core points, drawn red. The remaining points are non-core points, drawn green. Unvisited points are brown.
3.   grow a cluster from a random core point through core points within ε
We pick a core point at random and assign it to the cluster. Core points within ε of it join, then core points within ε of the growing cluster join in turn. The yellow circles mark the points whose neighborhoods are being searched.
4.   attach non-core points within ε of a core point in the cluster
These border points join the cluster without extending it. A border point in reach of two clusters joins whichever cluster grows first.
5.   repeat from an unclaimed core point, then label the leftovers noise
A finished cluster takes a fixed color of its own. Non-core points with no core point within ε become noise, drawn gray.

Conventions

This page counts neighbors without the point itself, as the slides do. scikit-learn's min_samples counts the point, so minPts = 4 here matches min_samples = 5 there.

DBSCAN needs no K. It needs ε and minPts, and the k-distance plot is the usual way to choose ε once minPts is fixed.

A border point within ε of core points from two clusters joins whichever cluster grows first, so the random starting point can decide its label. The core points and the noise come out the same in any order.
